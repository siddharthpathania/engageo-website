Someone calls at 9:42 PM with a toothache. The desk shut at seven. The phone rings out, they try the next clinic on their list, and by Tuesday that patient belongs to somebody else. None of it shows up in your revenue report; the loss never became a line item.

Every clinic owner ends up at the same question: what should happen to a call the desk cannot take? There are five real options, and each is genuinely better than the others at something, and genuinely worse at something else.

Judge each against the five things that decide it for an Indian clinic: cost, language, after-hours behaviour, patient experience, and what happens when a call is not routine.

## Start by counting what you actually miss

Pull two weeks of call logs from your telecom provider or your PBX. Sort them into four buckets: on another line during desk hours, nobody at the desk during desk hours, after closing, and Sundays and holidays. Mark which numbers rang you back on their own.

The buckets point at different fixes. Busy-line misses at 11:30 AM on a Tuesday are a capacity problem, and a second line plus a second person solves it cleanly. Misses at 9:42 PM are a coverage problem, and hiring rarely solves coverage economically. Sunday misses are a policy problem. And if most after-hours callers ring again at ten next morning, that bucket is smaller than it feels.

## More front-desk staff

A person is still the best thing that can happen to a difficult caller. They know which doctor is running late, they hear that a caregiver is upset, they settle a billing argument, and they handle the caller who fits no script.

The catch is that staff buy you hours, not calls. A second person on the existing shift fixes 11:30 AM properly. Extending cover to 10 PM means paying for a whole shift to catch a handful of calls, and the cost per answered call at the tail of the day is punishing. Add leave, festival season and notice periods. On language you get whatever you can hire locally, and that list is usually shorter than your catchment needs.

## A shared human answering service

A vendor's agents pick up in your clinic's name alongside their other clients. You pay for volume rather than a shift, and you get real humans at eleven at night. For message-taking and escalation, it works.

The agent knows only the script you wrote, so the script is the product, and quality varies with whoever picks up. Ask whether they book or only take messages: without access to your calendar an agent can promise a call-back, not a slot, and a promise is a second job for your desk next morning. Ask separately which languages are staffed at 10 PM, not which ones the website lists. Patient details are going to a third party, so put retention, access and deletion in the contract before the first call lands.

## Voicemail and the IVR menu

Voicemail is cheap, and it assumes something often untrue: that the caller will leave a message. Check how many voicemails your desk actually returns in a week. It earns a place as the last step after the caller is told when you open, not as the whole answer.

An IVR menu is better than its reputation at one job: routing. Research in primary care found IVR used effectively with groups that are hard to reach otherwise, including patients who are illiterate, have lower education, or do not speak the language it runs in, while those with hearing impairment have more difficulty and some people do not trust reporting information to a machine ([A scoping review to explore the suitability of interactive voice re…](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/primary-health-care-research-and-development/article/scoping-review-to-explore-the-suitability-of-interactive-voice-response-to-conduct-automated-performance-measurement-of-the-patients-experience-in-primary-care/36C25C9FFD323BE0406900C247C73BE9)).

The same work names two failure modes to design around. Callers frustrated by the pace will satisfice, picking the first available answer to move on, and others show a recency effect, choosing the option read out last ([A scoping review to explore the suitability of interactive voice re…](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/primary-health-care-research-and-development/article/scoping-review-to-explore-the-suitability-of-interactive-voice-response-to-conduct-automated-performance-measurement-of-the-patients-experience-in-primary-care/36C25C9FFD323BE0406900C247C73BE9)). So keep the menu short, put the commonest option first, and never nest a second menu inside the first. On cost, the review describes a substantial initial expense set against low incremental costs and long-term savings ([A scoping review to explore the suitability of interactive voice re…](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/primary-health-care-research-and-development/article/scoping-review-to-explore-the-suitability-of-interactive-voice-response-to-conduct-automated-performance-measurement-of-the-patients-experience-in-primary-care/36C25C9FFD323BE0406900C247C73BE9)).

What an IVR cannot do is book. It routes, and after hours there is nobody to route to.

## An AI voice agent

An AI voice agent can answer on the first ring at 9:42 PM, take several calls at once, say the same thing every time, and leave a transcript you can audit on Monday. Test one thing hard: whether it writes into your calendar or merely records an intention. Ask for a live call into a test clinic, then check the calendar.

The honest limits are audio and context. Accents, a two-wheeler going past, a caller switching languages mid-sentence. It will mishear a name. It cannot hear that someone is frightened. It does not know Dr Mehta stopped taking Thursday evenings unless told. Each is a configuration problem rather than a mystery, which is both the good news and the work. Ask what happens when the service is down at 9 PM, and get the fallback in writing. Engageo sits in this category, and so do several others.

A WhatsApp text-back usually sits underneath whichever option you pick, and the rules matter. A 24-hour customer service window opens when a WhatsApp user messages or calls your business account; inside it you may send free-form messages needing no pre-approval, outside it only pre-approved templates, and users must have opted in ([Service messages](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/messages/send-messages)). A missed call to your ordinary phone line does not open that window, so the first text-back needs an approved template ready.

## Language, after hours and safety

Language decides more of this than price does. The 2011 Census groups returns into 121 languages plus others, 22 of them scheduled ([Language Census Data](https://language.census.gov.in/showHomePage)). Your catchment is not 121 languages. Write down the mother tongue of your last fifty new patients. That short list, not the census, is your requirement. Ask each option what it covers at 9:42 PM, not at noon; the answers are rarely the same.

Safety is the trade-off nobody prices. A phone call is the thinnest clinical channel there is, and even trained clinicians misjudge it. A 2023 study of Danish out-of-hours telephone triage found clinically relevant undertriage in 5.5% of randomly selected calls and 7.9% of high-risk calls, rising to 13.2% for high-risk calls at night against 6.4% by day and evening ([Factors associated with undertriage and overtriage in telephone tri…](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10030474/)). Those were nurses and GPs.

The rule that follows applies to every option here. Whoever answers your phone, human or software, must not triage. No symptom questions, no judgement about whether something sounds serious, no advice. One line, the same way every time: if this is an emergency, go to your nearest hospital now or call 108. Under the National Health Mission, 35 States and Union Territories offer Dial 108 or 102: 108 predominantly for critical care, trauma and accident cases, 102 for basic patient transport, mainly pregnant women and children ([ERS/Patient Transport Service](https://nhm.gov.in/nhm_live/index1.php?lang=1&level=2&sublinkid=1217&lid=189)). Put that line in the receptionist's script, the answering service SOP, the IVR's opening and the agent's.

Decide what "handled" means before you shop. Three honest positions: the call is booked, the caller is promised a response by a stated time, or the caller is told when you open. Hold each option to one.

## Costing it without guessing

Do not compare monthly prices. Compare cost per answered call, with your own numbers.

- Take one bucket from your call log and count the calls the option would genuinely answer.
- Take the fully loaded monthly cost: salary plus statutory contributions plus training and cover for staff, or invoice plus usage charges plus setup spread over a year for a vendor.
- Divide one by the other.
- Then divide the value of one new patient by that figure: you now know how many extra bookings a month it must produce to pay for itself.

Run it separately per bucket. The winner is often different in each, and for many clinics the answer is two options rather than one.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I just divert after-hours calls to my own mobile?

You can, and plenty of owners do. It works until you are taking clinic calls at dinner and quietly stop picking up. A bridge while you count the buckets, not a system.

### Will patients accept talking to software?

Some will not, and that is worth designing for rather than arguing with. Make the first sentence say what it is, keep the exit to a human obvious, and listen to your own recordings in the first fortnight. Menus and machines have reached illiterate, lower-education and non-native-speaking patients well in primary care research, but trust is not universal ([A scoping review to explore the suitability of interactive voice re…](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/primary-health-care-research-and-development/article/scoping-review-to-explore-the-suitability-of-interactive-voice-response-to-conduct-automated-performance-measurement-of-the-patients-experience-in-primary-care/36C25C9FFD323BE0406900C247C73BE9)).

### Answering service or AI agent for a two-doctor clinic?

Low after-hours volume in a language the service staffs at night favours the human option, because you pay per call. Higher volume, several languages, or a need for booked slots rather than messages pushes towards the agent.

### What should I ask a vendor before signing?

Four things. Does it book into my calendar or only take a message. Which languages are live after 9 PM. What happens when it fails at night. Where patient data is stored, for how long, and how do I get it deleted.
