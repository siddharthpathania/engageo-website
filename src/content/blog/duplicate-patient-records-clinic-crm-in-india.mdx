In any busy Indian outpatient department (OPD), incoming calls and WhatsApp messages rarely follow a neat, one-patient-to-one-number rule. Family members routinely telephone on behalf of elderly parents, husbands book dermatology consultations for their wives, and mothers coordinate dental appointments for multiple children using a single personal SIM card. At the same time, an adult child might call from an alternate office number or a separate WhatsApp account to reschedule their parent's follow-up visit. When front-desk staff or default booking tools treat the incoming caller ID as the primary patient key, this everyday reality silently fractures your clinic's database.

Defaulting to incoming telephone numbers as unique record identifiers creates scattered, duplicate patient charts across practice management systems and CRMs. A returning chronic care or post-operative patient suddenly has two or three separate files: one tied to their spouse's phone number, one to their daughter's WhatsApp account, and another to their own device. As a result, critical consultation notes, medication adjustments, and past diagnostic reports are separated across disconnected profiles.

The repercussions extend beyond administrative confusion. Patient matching errors disconnect a patient's current visit from their prior clinical documentation, directly elevating the risk of improper care, misplaced medical history, and redundant diagnostic procedures ([A Realistic Approach to Achieving a 1% Duplicate Record Error Rate](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQF2-pCNT030t88Ca3ti4BgmhC1KjMJ8iBFN-2Ak52LRAlqlaiORqYT-SMlqvoNbmVl3pZjoxB-ybJiJ8bKkiSfQJ6Pyxeh6yC_WY0Ubg8zAemjmmKnBlN8lygYZ51Gr3Ap0rvYZUnQhHSrQQb47tmc=)). When clinicians cannot review earlier scans or blood panels, patients undergo repeated testing, leading to unnecessary blood draws and unwarranted diagnostic radiation exposure ([Decade of Standardization: Data Integrity as a Foundation for Trust…](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFDpcmxpu2CMc-5RVKL2XrO1xzEVUgiTKiWGpXSqGsQHZpX1cvnMvJbwxPLUwM3pRIuaVk2jliyP9DlbDUQp8s1QjMQ95w2bYeOva_cBIC8JVeFW3_Q9XvzuEZjjvrw6AJqHAqdm5U4kIN7MEPLeXJPa4Utqf_diLengpbgIHzmXvRj_2e2Ue1v_H7iWjn1ye5MQpzLZFRDZ73K8tveIvPSHf3iPDcZHHLICea9An0icld2Y5V7RxAv6c79R8zLVIz4EQfzfA8RTYL9z0hZxY5Ojvf4XnkfTaE1IAYO8IcEegvxumkxBzYN9CfDwb4ogamqwYQ=)). To safeguard patient safety and streamline daily operations, Indian practices must transition from phone-number-centric tracking to a structured, multi-identifier intake system across both automated channels and manual reception workflows.

## Why Shared Family Numbers Create Operational Chaos in Indian Clinic CRMs

The multi-member household dynamic in India makes phone numbers inherently unreliable as unique identifiers. In many families, one digitally active individual handles healthcare appointments, hospital visits, and billing for parents, in-laws, and children alike. When that family member contacts your clinic, standard CRM workflows that auto-generate a new contact record for every unrecognised phone number immediately create duplicate files for returning patients.

National health authorities have explicitly acknowledged this fundamental data challenge. Official guidelines under the [Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM)](https://abdm.gov.in/faq/aarogyasetuapp) emphasise that a mobile number cannot uniquely authenticate an individual, which is why system architectures that rely strictly on mobile numbers permit multiple profiles to be created for the same citizen until additional official identity verification is integrated ([FAQs - Frequently Asked Questions | ABDM | NHA](https://abdm.gov.in/faq/aarogyasetuapp)). In a clinic setting, assigning medical history to a caller ID ignores this structural reality.

When intake software creates a separate profile every time an inquiry arrives from an unfamiliar family SIM, your doctors lose visibility of longitudinal patient history during subsequent OPD consultations. A clinician reviewing an incomplete chart may miss past drug allergies, ongoing treatment regimens, or previous procedural notes recorded under a relative's profile. The operational and clinical impact of this fragmentation is widespread:

- In institutional surveys, 86 percent of healthcare respondents reported witnessing or having direct knowledge of a medical error caused by patient misidentification ([A Realistic Approach to Achieving a 1% Duplicate Record Error Rate](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQF2-pCNT030t88Ca3ti4BgmhC1KjMJ8iBFN-2Ak52LRAlqlaiORqYT-SMlqvoNbmVl3pZjoxB-ybJiJ8bKkiSfQJ6Pyxeh6yC_WY0Ubg8zAemjmmKnBlN8lygYZ51Gr3Ap0rvYZUnQhHSrQQb47tmc=)).
- At Children's Medical Center of Dallas, an audit revealed that 45 percent of medical staff routinely encountered duplicate medical record numbers, with 25 percent confirming that these duplicates actively impaired healthcare delivery ([Decade of Standardization: Data Integrity as a Foundation for Trust…](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFDpcmxpu2CMc-5RVKL2XrO1xzEVUgiTKiWGpXSqGsQHZpX1cvnMvJbwxPLUwM3pRIuaVk2jliyP9DlbDUQp8s1QjMQ95w2bYeOva_cBIC8JVeFW3_Q9XvzuEZjjvrw6AJqHAqdm5U4kIN7MEPLeXJPa4Utqf_diLengpbgIHzmXvRj_2e2Ue1v_H7iWjn1ye5MQpzLZFRDZ73K8tveIvPSHf3iPDcZHHLICea9An0icld2Y5V7RxAv6c79R8zLVIz4EQfzfA8RTYL9z0hZxY5Ojvf4XnkfTaE1IAYO8IcEegvxumkxBzYN9CfDwb4ogamqwYQ=)).
- Even healthcare organisations that successfully manage their duplicate record error rate down to 1 percent still leave thousands of patient charts at ongoing risk of misdiagnosis, redundant lab work, and clinical missteps ([A Realistic Approach to Achieving a 1% Duplicate Record Error Rate](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQF2-pCNT030t88Ca3ti4BgmhC1KjMJ8iBFN-2Ak52LRAlqlaiORqYT-SMlqvoNbmVl3pZjoxB-ybJiJ8bKkiSfQJ6Pyxeh6yC_WY0Ubg8zAemjmmKnBlN8lygYZ51Gr3Ap0rvYZUnQhHSrQQb47tmc=)).

Treating an incoming phone number as a patient's primary database key creates an unmanageable trail of orphaned records. Indian clinics must recognise phone numbers for what they actually are: convenient communication endpoints, not unique clinical identities.

## Identifier Architecture: Moving Beyond Mobile Numbers in Outpatient Intake

To eliminate duplicate patient charts, clinics need an identifier architecture rooted in established national healthcare standards rather than generic commercial sales CRM templates. Regulatory and accreditation bodies in India provide clear frameworks for establishing robust patient identification protocols that withstand proxy bookings.

Accreditation standards set by the National Accreditation Board for Hospitals and Healthcare Providers (NABH) require that every medical record possess a unique identifier ([Self Assessment Toolkit - NABH](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFZTqiAGcg4tms2ndIeFkwz5qPK2F1cxvJEiqVIaHhcjNgI5UqdUQs5T9lc4PSd_BhsNMBjX5nqTk5a7dkIJmrNOavz--6gXFVH6nMmZTlfIO9FV8_2n3OySOWVikcHPsQDig==)). Furthermore, [NABH standard AAC.2](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFSKleJm1HU_ubfE58QXGvLngcnMwuqedXTYI7pwf6svziyguw01ECXw38SpaxTLlUEZtZ67gzYAn9AH-ZcdWLrxJETq_ZNL4dI8iO0koCF4h5JYSLPO0fZFD8lseE309PeZcHXr3ReEzTsNhh6CnfpYAUo0vSqtHIxOqfUTgB1TDqlO7p6YsbE1Voewg==) mandates that a unique identification number be generated at the conclusion of patient registration ([NABH ACCREDITATION STANDARDS FOR HOSPITALS (5th edition)](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFSKleJm1HU_ubfE58QXGvLngcnMwuqedXTYI7pwf6svziyguw01ECXw38SpaxTLlUEZtZ67gzYAn9AH-ZcdWLrxJETq_ZNL4dI8iO0koCF4h5JYSLPO0fZFD8lseE309PeZcHXr3ReEzTsNhh6CnfpYAUo0vSqtHIxOqfUTgB1TDqlO7p6YsbE1Voewg==)), while clinical standard COP.1 requires healthcare organisations to enforce a uniform identification process using a minimum of two distinct identifiers ([NABH ACCREDITATION STANDARDS FOR HOSPITALS (5th edition)](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFSKleJm1HU_ubfE58QXGvLngcnMwuqedXTYI7pwf6svziyguw01ECXw38SpaxTLlUEZtZ67gzYAn9AH-ZcdWLrxJETq_ZNL4dI8iO0koCF4h5JYSLPO0fZFD8lseE309PeZcHXr3ReEzTsNhh6CnfpYAUo0vSqtHIxOqfUTgB1TDqlO7p6YsbE1Voewg==)). Relying solely on caller ID fails to meet these accreditation baselines.

The Ministry of Health & Family Welfare's [Electronic Health Record (EHR) Standards for India](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQHjt81WMa_YW0UhHvSFzJpZ3VzKAGKupXIGR4t485fHxJ9lEik2lcsAfwewRKxtgZj9bWh4okZH3lSHvxDjz7yDOjefoUts83dhK805ZTKFcdAmsF_flwez8lozrwIBQ4bT-05lAqfuPiW2wa2jyEC02XV8QIZoAA==) recommend establishing a dedicated Patient Unique Identifier to anchor and link all clinical artifacts and diagnostic records across visits ([ELECTRONIC HEALTH RECORD (EHR) STANDARDS FOR INDIA (2016) Standards…](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQHjt81WMa_YW0UhHvSFzJpZ3VzKAGKupXIGR4t485fHxJ9lEik2lcsAfwewRKxtgZj9bWh4okZH3lSHvxDjz7yDOjefoUts83dhK805ZTKFcdAmsF_flwez8lozrwIBQ4bT-05lAqfuPiW2wa2jyEC02XV8QIZoAA==)). Under these standards, the UIDAI Aadhaar number is preferred when available; however, because legal provisions restrict Aadhaar from being mandatory for health registration, facilities must utilise a local identifier alongside any central or state government photo identity card ([FAQs - Frequently Asked Questions | ABDM | NHA](https://abdm.gov.in/faq/aarogyasetuapp), [ELECTRONIC HEALTH RECORD (EHR) STANDARDS FOR INDIA (2016) Standards…](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQHjt81WMa_YW0UhHvSFzJpZ3VzKAGKupXIGR4t485fHxJ9lEik2lcsAfwewRKxtgZj9bWh4okZH3lSHvxDjz7yDOjefoUts83dhK805ZTKFcdAmsF_flwez8lozrwIBQ4bT-05lAqfuPiW2wa2jyEC02XV8QIZoAA==)).

On a national scale, the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM) addresses fragmented health tracking by creating 14-digit ABHA Numbers and core citizen registries, giving patients a single account to consolidate their complete longitudinal medical records across disparate public and private facilities ([FAQs - Frequently Asked Questions | ABDM | NHA](https://abdm.gov.in/faq/aarogyasetuapp)). Patients can access their records via the ABHA App using their 14-digit ABHA Number, their ABHA Address and password, their mobile number, or their email address ([FAQs - Frequently Asked Questions | ABDM | NHA](https://abdm.gov.in/faq/aarogyasetuapp)).

For private outpatient clinics, your internal CRM or Hospital Management System (HMS) must decouple the communication channel from the clinical ledger. Configure your database schema around the following primary and secondary parameters:

- Primary Master Key: A system-generated Unique Clinic Patient ID (such as an internal MRN), linked where possible to the patient's 14-digit ABHA Number.
- Mandatory Secondary Identifiers: Full legal name, date of birth (or verified year of birth), and gender, fulfilling the NABH requirement for two distinct patient identifiers ([NABH ACCREDITATION STANDARDS FOR HOSPITALS (5th edition)](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFSKleJm1HU_ubfE58QXGvLngcnMwuqedXTYI7pwf6svziyguw01ECXw38SpaxTLlUEZtZ67gzYAn9AH-ZcdWLrxJETq_ZNL4dI8iO0koCF4h5JYSLPO0fZFD8lseE309PeZcHXr3ReEzTsNhh6CnfpYAUo0vSqtHIxOqfUTgB1TDqlO7p6YsbE1Voewg==)).
- Communication Endpoints (Many-to-One): An array of associated contact numbers labelled by relationship (e.g., 'Primary Self', 'Son\\'s Mobile', 'Spouse WhatsApp') linked to the master clinical profile.
- Government Photo ID Field: A dedicated field for alternate official IDs when Aadhaar is not provided, adhering to national EHR recommendations ([ELECTRONIC HEALTH RECORD (EHR) STANDARDS FOR INDIA (2016) Standards…](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQHjt81WMa_YW0UhHvSFzJpZ3VzKAGKupXIGR4t485fHxJ9lEik2lcsAfwewRKxtgZj9bWh4okZH3lSHvxDjz7yDOjefoUts83dhK805ZTKFcdAmsF_flwez8lozrwIBQ4bT-05lAqfuPiW2wa2jyEC02XV8QIZoAA==)).

## Structuring Voice and WhatsApp Intake to Resolve Patient Identity

The front door of an Indian clinic is increasingly digital, handled via automated WhatsApp messaging and telephony routing. If these intake systems immediately create a new database profile whenever an unfamiliar telephone number rings, your software will generate hundreds of duplicates each month. Structuring automated intake flows requires intentional identity resolution before a booking is confirmed.

The first step in any conversational intake flow—whether powered by voice AI or WhatsApp automation—is to establish proxy context. Before asking for appointment slots, the system must prompt: 'Are you booking this consultation for yourself or a family member?' If the caller indicates they are booking on behalf of someone else, the intake workflow must split into a structured proxy pathway rather than assigning the caller's identity to the appointment slot.

When a proxy pathway is triggered, or when an unfamiliar caller claims to be a returning patient, automated intake systems must request secondary verification attributes before searching the database:

1. Step 1 (Relationship Capture): Capture the caller's relationship to the patient (parent, child, spouse, attendant).
2. Step 2 (Demographic Query): Ask for the patient's registered full name and year of birth, or their existing clinic patient ID.
3. Step 3 (Match & Confirmation): Match these inputs against existing CRM profiles. If a unique record matches, the system attaches the new booking to that historical record while recording the incoming number as an authorised secondary contact.
4. Step 4 (Disambiguation): If multiple potential matches appear, route the interaction to a front-desk verification queue instead of auto-creating a new record.

During morning OPD peaks, clinics routinely miss dozens of calls while staff attend to in-person queues. Implementing missed call recovery systems with instant WhatsApp text-back links helps recover these patients without creating duplicate profiles. The automated WhatsApp booking link must explicitly ask returning patients to verify their existing profile using their registered name or clinic ID, rather than blindly registering the caller ID as a fresh lead.

Language barriers frequently compound proxy booking errors. Elderly relatives or tier-2 city callers often struggle with rigid English interactive voice response (IVR) menus, leading them to press arbitrary keys or provide truncated names. Deploying conversational voice reception in regional Indian languages—such as Hindi or Marathi alongside Indian English—ensures callers clearly understand proxy questions and can accurately state the registered patient's full details.

## Front-Desk Protocols: Verification Rules for Rapid Phone Intake

Even with automated tools in place, clinic receptionists field hundreds of direct phone inquiries daily. Under the time pressure of ringing phones and waiting OPD queues, front-desk staff often default to the fastest path in the software: clicking 'Create New Patient' and entering whatever number appears on caller ID. Preventing duplicates requires a disciplined standard operating procedure (SOP).

Front-desk teams should be trained on a mandatory 15-second verification script before creating any new profile. When answering an incoming inquiry, the receptionist must ask:

'Have you or the patient visited our clinic before? Could you please confirm the patient's full registered name and date of birth?'

Staff must execute a search in the clinic CRM using the patient's legal name and birth year before initiating a new registration. Rigorous intake controls produce dramatic improvements in data quality: in an institutional [study documented by the Journal of AHIMA](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFDpcmxpu2CMc-5RVKL2XrO1xzEVUgiTKiWGpXSqGsQHZpX1cvnMvJbwxPLUwM3pRIuaVk2jliyP9DlbDUQp8s1QjMQ95w2bYeOva_cBIC8JVeFW3_Q9XvzuEZjjvrw6AJqHAqdm5U4kIN7MEPLeXJPa4Utqf_diLengpbgIHzmXvRj_2e2Ue1v_H7iWjn1ye5MQpzLZFRDZ73K8tveIvPSHf3iPDcZHHLICea9An0icld2Y5V7RxAv6c79R8zLVIz4EQfzfA8RTYL9z0hZxY5Ojvf4XnkfTaE1IAYO8IcEegvxumkxBzYN9CfDwb4ogamqwYQ=), implementing standardised naming conventions, technical intake forcing functions, and regular error feedback reduced a facility's duplicate record rate from 22 percent to below 0.2 percent ([Decade of Standardization: Data Integrity as a Foundation for Trust…](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFDpcmxpu2CMc-5RVKL2XrO1xzEVUgiTKiWGpXSqGsQHZpX1cvnMvJbwxPLUwM3pRIuaVk2jliyP9DlbDUQp8s1QjMQ95w2bYeOva_cBIC8JVeFW3_Q9XvzuEZjjvrw6AJqHAqdm5U4kIN7MEPLeXJPa4Utqf_diLengpbgIHzmXvRj_2e2Ue1v_H7iWjn1ye5MQpzLZFRDZ73K8tveIvPSHf3iPDcZHHLICea9An0icld2Y5V7RxAv6c79R8zLVIz4EQfzfA8RTYL9z0hZxY5Ojvf4XnkfTaE1IAYO8IcEegvxumkxBzYN9CfDwb4ogamqwYQ=)).

To maintain staff accountability, NABH standards require that every medical record entry be dated and timed, and that the author of each entry can be clearly identified ([Self Assessment Toolkit - NABH](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFZTqiAGcg4tms2ndIeFkwz5qPK2F1cxvJEiqVIaHhcjNgI5UqdUQs5T9lc4PSd_BhsNMBjX5nqTk5a7dkIJmrNOavz--6gXFVH6nMmZTlfIO9FV8_2n3OySOWVikcHPsQDig==)). Receptionists should operate under individual user credentials rather than shared front-desk logins. This ensures practice managers can trace duplicate entries back to specific shifts, assess training gaps, and reinforce intake compliance without disrupting daily appointment flow.

## Merging Duplicate Consultation Ledgers Without Losing Clinical Notes

Preventing new duplicates handles future intake, but most established practices already carry databases cluttered with split histories. Cleaning up duplicate consultation ledgers requires strict adherence to data governance principles to ensure clinical continuity is preserved without compromising medical integrity.

The Electronic Health Record Standards for India explicitly mandate that healthcare software systems provide search, merge, and demerge features while maintaining a comprehensive audit trail ([ELECTRONIC HEALTH RECORD (EHR) STANDARDS FOR INDIA (2016) Standards…](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQHjt81WMa_YW0UhHvSFzJpZ3VzKAGKupXIGR4t485fHxJ9lEik2lcsAfwewRKxtgZj9bWh4okZH3lSHvxDjz7yDOjefoUts83dhK805ZTKFcdAmsF_flwez8lozrwIBQ4bT-05lAqfuPiW2wa2jyEC02XV8QIZoAA==)). Under no circumstances should reconciling duplicate records result in the deletion of previous doctor notes, billing entries, or prescription timestamps.

When executing record reconciliation, clinic administrators must follow key compliance rules:

- Data Immutability and Versioning: Once clinical data is entered into a health record system, it must remain immutable; any merge or update must preserve the original record through versioning and an audit trail rather than overwriting historical files ([ELECTRONIC HEALTH RECORD (EHR) STANDARDS FOR INDIA (2016) Standards…](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQHjt81WMa_YW0UhHvSFzJpZ3VzKAGKupXIGR4t485fHxJ9lEik2lcsAfwewRKxtgZj9bWh4okZH3lSHvxDjz7yDOjefoUts83dhK805ZTKFcdAmsF_flwez8lozrwIBQ4bT-05lAqfuPiW2wa2jyEC02XV8QIZoAA==)).
- Documented Security Procedures: NABH standards require healthcare facilities to maintain documented policies and procedures for preserving the confidentiality, integrity, and security of patient data throughout record handling ([Self Assessment Toolkit - NABH](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFZTqiAGcg4tms2ndIeFkwz5qPK2F1cxvJEiqVIaHhcjNgI5UqdUQs5T9lc4PSd_BhsNMBjX5nqTk5a7dkIJmrNOavz--6gXFVH6nMmZTlfIO9FV8_2n3OySOWVikcHPsQDig==)).
- Non-Destruction of Records: National EHR standards stipulate that health records must be preserved throughout an individual's lifetime and never permanently destroyed during administrative deduplication ([ELECTRONIC HEALTH RECORD (EHR) STANDARDS FOR INDIA (2016) Standards…](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQHjt81WMa_YW0UhHvSFzJpZ3VzKAGKupXIGR4t485fHxJ9lEik2lcsAfwewRKxtgZj9bWh4okZH3lSHvxDjz7yDOjefoUts83dhK805ZTKFcdAmsF_flwez8lozrwIBQ4bT-05lAqfuPiW2wa2jyEC02XV8QIZoAA==)).
- Retention Governance: Clinics must maintain documented procedures for record retention and data custody, ensuring historical ledgers remain accessible across the patient's care timeline ([Self Assessment Toolkit - NABH](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFZTqiAGcg4tms2ndIeFkwz5qPK2F1cxvJEiqVIaHhcjNgI5UqdUQs5T9lc4PSd_BhsNMBjX5nqTk5a7dkIJmrNOavz--6gXFVH6nMmZTlfIO9FV8_2n3OySOWVikcHPsQDig==)).

To systematically clean your database, adopt the cyclic 'ICMMR' methodology recommended by the American Health Information Management Association (AHIMA), which guides healthcare organisations through five iterative stages: Identifying, Cleaning, Measuring, Mitigating, and Remediating duplicate records ([A Realistic Approach to Achieving a 1% Duplicate Record Error Rate](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQF2-pCNT030t88Ca3ti4BgmhC1KjMJ8iBFN-2Ak52LRAlqlaiORqYT-SMlqvoNbmVl3pZjoxB-ybJiJ8bKkiSfQJ6Pyxeh6yC_WY0Ubg8zAemjmmKnBlN8lygYZ51Gr3Ap0rvYZUnQhHSrQQb47tmc=)). Running monthly deduplication audits allows clinic managers to flag matching demographic sets, merge split consultation ledgers into a single primary record, and maintain an uncompromised longitudinal chart for every patient.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do phone numbers cause duplicate records in Indian clinic CRMs?

In India, family members frequently book appointments for relatives using a single SIM card or alternating contact numbers. CRMs that treat caller ID as the primary patient identifier auto-generate a new record whenever an unfamiliar number calls, splitting a returning patient's medical history across multiple profiles.

### What identifiers should Indian clinics use instead of mobile numbers?

Clinics should decouple communication numbers from clinical files by assigning a master internal Patient ID or linking an ABHA Number. Following NABH standards, intake systems should mandate at least two distinct identifiers, such as legal name, date or year of birth, and gender.

### How can clinics prevent duplicates during automated WhatsApp and phone booking?

Automated voice and WhatsApp workflows should ask upfront whether the booking is for the caller or a family member. When a proxy booking is detected, the workflow must capture the patient's full name, year of birth, or existing clinic ID to match existing records before scheduling.

### What rules apply when merging duplicate medical records under Indian standards?

National EHR standards require healthcare systems to maintain complete audit trails and record immutability during merges. Clinical data such as consultation notes, billing timestamps, and prescriptions must be preserved through versioning and never permanently destroyed.
