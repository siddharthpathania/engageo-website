Picture a call that reaches a fertility clinic late in the evening. The caller does not give her name. She asks what a first consultation costs, then whether the clinic is discreet, then whether anyone will call back on this number. She rings off before the receptionist finishes answering.

That kind of enquiry is among the hardest to hold on to. The caller has often told nobody. The handset may be shared with a husband, a mother-in-law, a sister who opens notifications. A missed call from an unknown number, or a WhatsApp preview on a locked screen, can end the enquiry before it starts.

Little of what follows is a marketing idea. Most is operating discipline, and much is already settled by law.

## What the law has already decided

The Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act, 2021 puts confidentiality on the clinic, not on the patient. Clinics and banks must ensure that information about the commissioning couple, woman and donor is kept confidential, and information about treatment must not be disclosed to anyone except the database maintained by the National Registry, in a medical emergency at the request of the commissioning couple to whom the information relates, or by an order of a court of competent jurisdiction ([The Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act, 2021 (No. 42…](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2021/The%20Assisted%20Reproductive%20Technology%20%28Regulation%29%20Act,%202021.pdf)). There is no fourth exception. Not a husband asking about his wife's appointment, not a referring doctor, not a worried relative.

The Act sets no separate penalty for that duty, so the residual provision applies: a contravention with no specific punishment attracts a fine of five to ten lakh rupees for a first offence, and three to eight years with a ten to twenty lakh rupee fine for a subsequent one ([The Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act, 2021 (No. 42…](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2021/The%20Assisted%20Reproductive%20Technology%20%28Regulation%29%20Act,%202021.pdf)). Where a clinic commits the offence, its executive head is deemed guilty unless he proves due diligence ([The Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act, 2021 (No. 42…](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2021/The%20Assisted%20Reproductive%20Technology%20%28Regulation%29%20Act,%202021.pdf)).

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 governs the enquiry record itself. A consent request must be accompanied or preceded by a notice stating what data you collect, for what purpose, how she exercises her rights and how she complains to the Board ([The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/3510545/)). Consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, given by a clear affirmative action, and limited to the data necessary for that purpose ([The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/3510545/)). She can withdraw it as easily as she gave it.

One line matters for whoever writes your campaigns: no clinic, bank or agent may advertise, in any manner including the internet, facilities of sex selective assisted reproductive technology ([The Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act, 2021 (No. 42…](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2021/The%20Assisted%20Reproductive%20Technology%20%28Regulation%29%20Act,%202021.pdf)).

## The first ninety seconds on the phone

Whoever picks up is not there to assess anything. They are there to make the caller feel safe enough to leave a number.

- Give the clinic name and your own first name. Do not ask for a full name yet.
- Ask one question before any other: is this number safe to call back on, and when can you speak freely? Write the answer down, and let it govern everything that follows.
- Ask which language she would prefer for the call back.
- Answer money questions plainly. A consultation fee is not confidential, and refusing to state it reads as evasion.
- Do not ask about cycles, test results, previous attempts, or the husband's reports. A receptionist asking clinical questions is both useless and alarming.
- If the caller describes something urgent, stop and tell her to seek emergency care straight away. Do not triage on the phone.

Two boundaries save arguments later. The Act permits services to a woman above twenty-one and below fifty, and a man above twenty-one and below fifty-five ([The Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act, 2021 (No. 42…](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2021/The%20Assisted%20Reproductive%20Technology%20%28Regulation%29%20Act,%202021.pdf)), so promise no appointment that cannot legally end in treatment. And the counselling the Act requires, on implications and chances of success, and on costs, side effects and risks, is professional counselling the clinic provides ([The Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act, 2021 (No. 42…](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2021/The%20Assisted%20Reproductive%20Technology%20%28Regulation%29%20Act,%202021.pdf)), not anything a first phone call should attempt.

## Who calls back, and how soon

The person who calls back should be a named counsellor or patient coordinator, the same one every time, introduced by name in the first message. Not a rotating pool, and not a doctor at this stage unless she asked. Every later call then comes from a name she already recognises.

Speed matters less than keeping the promise. A call inside the window she chose beats a faster call at a moment that exposes her. If she gave no window, call once within the working day, leave no voicemail, and send a neutral text instead.

Record who initiated what. Under India's telemedicine practice guidelines, consent is implied when the patient initiates the consultation, but when the practitioner initiates it, explicit consent in words the patient understands must be obtained and noted in the records ([Telemedicine practice guidelines in India: global implications in t…](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9111269/)). Audio, video and text are all recognised modes, and the identities of both patient and practitioner must be established before the consultation begins ([Telemedicine practice guidelines in India: global implications in t…](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9111269/)). So the line in your system should read: patient called Tuesday 11:30 AM, consented to a call back after 8 PM on this number, spoke to Dr Mehta Thursday.

If an automated system answers after hours, as clinics using tools like Engageo do, hold it to the same rule. It may confirm that a person will call, capture a safe time and a language, nothing else.

## What never goes into a WhatsApp message

WhatsApp is a convenient channel for this audience and the wrong place for most of the content. The Business Messaging Policy is explicit: do not use WhatsApp for telemedicine or to send or request health related information where applicable regulations prohibit distributing it to systems without heightened safeguards ([WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)). It also bars asking people to share full length payment card numbers, financial account numbers, personal ID card numbers or other sensitive identifiers, and forbids forwarding information from one customer chat to another ([WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)).

Keep these out of every outbound message:

- The words IVF, ICSI, infertility, fertility or donor in the first line, because that is what shows on a locked screen.
- Any test result, diagnosis, medication name, or the reason for the appointment.
- Aadhaar, PAN, insurance or card numbers, in either direction ([WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)).
- Anything that could read as advertising sex selection ([The Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act, 2021 (No. 42…](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2021/The%20Assisted%20Reproductive%20Technology%20%28Regulation%29%20Act,%202021.pdf)).
- Forwarded screenshots of another patient's chat or report ([WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)).

What is safe: your clinic name, a named person, a date and time, a direction and a way to reschedule. "Sunrise Clinic: your appointment with Dr Mehta is confirmed for Tuesday 11:30 AM. Reply 2 to change it."

The mechanics matter too. You may only message someone who gave you their number and gave opt-in permission to receive messages from you ([WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)), and outside the customer service window only an approved template can be sent ([Message Templates, WhatsApp Business Platform, Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/business-management-api/message-templates)). Templates fall into authentication, marketing and utility categories, are reviewed automatically on creation or edit, and cannot be sent if rejected ([Message Templates, WhatsApp Business Platform, Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/business-management-api/message-templates)). Get reminder and reschedule templates approved before you need them, and keep promotional sends on a separate number.

## Keeping records without exposing them

Split the enquiry log from the medical record. It needs a name or an initial, a number, a preferred language, a safe window and the source, not a reason for the enquiry. Anything clinical belongs in the clinical system, where fewer people hold logins.

Then apply the two retention regimes, because they pull in opposite directions. Records under the ART Act must be kept for at least ten years and then transferred to the National Registry's central database, preserved longer if proceedings are on foot, and made available for inspection at all reasonable times ([The Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act, 2021 (No. 42…](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2021/The%20Assisted%20Reproductive%20Technology%20%28Regulation%29%20Act,%202021.pdf)). Personal data collected on consent must be erased once consent is withdrawn or the purpose is no longer served, unless a law requires you to keep it ([The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/3510545/)). In practice the treatment record stays and the marketing list does not. An old enquiry that never converted has no business sitting in a broadcast audience.

Three habits close the common holes:

1. No clinic conversation on a personal phone or personal WhatsApp account. When a coordinator resigns, the chat history leaves on her handset.
2. Give the enquiry log and the clinical system different access lists, and review both on a fixed schedule.
3. Rehearse a breach. Under the DPDP Act you must take reasonable security safeguards and, if a breach occurs, give intimation to the Board and to each affected person ([The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/3510545/)).

Both laws expect a complaints route. Every clinic and bank must maintain a grievance cell ([The Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act, 2021 (No. 42…](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2021/The%20Assisted%20Reproductive%20Technology%20%28Regulation%29%20Act,%202021.pdf)), and a data fiduciary must run an effective mechanism to redress grievances ([The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/3510545/)). Publish one number and one email, and make sure somebody reads that inbox.

## Frequently asked questions

### A husband calls asking about his wife's appointment. Can we confirm it?

No. The exceptions are the National Registry, a medical emergency at the request of the commissioning couple to whom the information relates, and a court order ([The Assisted Reproductive Technology (Regulation) Act, 2021 (No. 42…](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2021/The%20Assisted%20Reproductive%20Technology%20%28Regulation%29%20Act,%202021.pdf)). Take his number, say the clinic can only speak to the person whose record it is, and call her.

### Can we send test results on WhatsApp if the patient asks us to?

Treat that as a no by default. The messaging policy warns against sending or requesting health related information where regulations restrict its distribution ([WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)). Send a note that a report is ready and hand it over in a portal or in person.

### Which language should we write in?

The one she asked for. The DPDP Act gives a person the option to read the notice in English or any language in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution ([The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/3510545/)), so capture the preference on the first call and keep it with the record.
