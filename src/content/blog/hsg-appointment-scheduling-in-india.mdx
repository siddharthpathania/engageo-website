For gynaecology and fertility clinics across India, hysterosalpingography (HSG) is a vital diagnostic test. Yet it creates constant operational hurdles for front-desk teams. Unlike routine outpatient consultations, antenatal visits, or ultrasound scans, clinics cannot book an HSG weeks in advance. The timing of the test depends directly on the patient's cycle. Clinical guidelines recommend [performing an HSG in the first half of the menstrual cycle](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/hysterosalpingography), between days 1 and 14, to reduce the chance that the patient might be pregnant ([Hysterosalpingography (HSG)](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/hysterosalpingography)).

This biological schedule creates an administrative bottleneck. When a patient starts her period, she has only a short window to contact the clinic and secure a slot. In busy Indian outpatient departments (OPDs), receptionists handle patient billing, registration queues, walk-in enquiries, and constant phone calls. If an enquiry goes unanswered, the patient may miss her monthly diagnostic window. For couples undergoing fertility workups, missing this window delays their care by an entire month.

Managing HSG appointment scheduling requires replacing manual telephone tag with automated intake. This guide provides an operational blueprint for Indian gynaecology clinics and fertility centres. It shows how to capture cycle dates automatically, coordinate shared diagnostic suites between gynaecologists and radiologists, deliver pre-procedure instructions over WhatsApp, and protect staff time.

## Why Standard Booking Fails for HSG Appointment Scheduling

Most clinic practice management systems rely on static calendars. They assume receptionists can distribute appointments evenly across working hours without clinical checks. Applying this standard calendar model to time-sensitive diagnostic tests causes frequent scheduling conflicts, empty procedure rooms, and frustrated patients.

Clinical guidelines dictate strict scheduling parameters. An HSG is best performed during days 1 to 14 of the menstrual cycle, specifically in the first half ([Hysterosalpingography (HSG)](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/hysterosalpingography)). The procedure is usually performed [after the menstrual period ends but before ovulation occurs](https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/hysterosalpingogram-hsg/) ([Hysterosalpingogram (HSG)](https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/hysterosalpingogram-hsg/)). Ideally, healthcare providers schedule the test [between cycle days 7 and 10](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diagnostics/22254-hysterosalpingogram) ([Hysterosalpingogram (HSG): Preparation, Procedure & Results](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diagnostics/22254-hysterosalpingogram)). This precise timeframe minimises the chance of an undetected pregnancy during the examination ([Hysterosalpingography (HSG)](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/hysterosalpingography), [Hysterosalpingogram (HSG): Preparation, Procedure & Results](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diagnostics/22254-hysterosalpingogram)).

Clinical protocols also establish several clear situations where an HSG must not be conducted ([Hysterosalpingography (HSG)](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/hysterosalpingography), [Hysterosalpingogram (HSG)](https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/hysterosalpingogram-hsg/)):

- Suspected pregnancy: An HSG should not be done if pregnancy is suspected ([Hysterosalpingogram (HSG)](https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/hysterosalpingogram-hsg/)). Clinics may administer a urine pregnancy test before the procedure to confirm ([Hysterosalpingography (HSG)](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/hysterosalpingography)).
- Active uterine bleeding: An HSG is not performed if the patient has uterine bleeding at the time of the procedure ([Hysterosalpingography (HSG)](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/hysterosalpingography)).
- Active pelvic infection: The procedure must not be conducted if the patient has a pelvic infection ([Hysterosalpingography (HSG)](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/hysterosalpingography)).
- Contrast dye allergies: An HSG should not be carried out if the patient has a moderate to severe contrast dye allergy ([Hysterosalpingography (HSG)](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/hysterosalpingography)). In addition, patients allergic to iodine require an HSG performed with contrast media that does not contain iodine ([Hysterosalpingogram (HSG)](https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/hysterosalpingogram-hsg/)).

When front desks assign dates manually without checking the patient's cycle stage, clinics experience high rates of day-of-procedure cancellations. A patient arrives at the clinic only for staff to discover that she is still bleeding or has already ovulated. The fluoroscopy room sits idle, specialist time is wasted, and the patient must wait for her next cycle.

## Automating Cycle-Start Capture and Dynamic Window Matching

To prevent cancellations, clinics need an intake protocol that records the start of menses immediately and calculates valid appointment dates. Relying on patients to call during clinic hours forces receptionists to perform mental date math while handling busy queues.

Gynaecologists routinely instruct patients to contact the OPD on 'Day 1'—the first day of full menstrual bleeding. However, periods frequently begin during late evenings, weekends, or early mornings before clinic doors open. When a patient calls and meets a busy signal or an unanswered phone line, she may delay reaching out or contact another diagnostic centre. In private practice, an unanswered call often turns into a lost patient.

An automated intake channel solves this problem through missed call recovery and instant messaging. When a front desk misses a call, the system triggers an immediate missed call text-back on WhatsApp or SMS. The patient completes a short intake sequence designed with dynamic date matching:

- Menstrual onset capture: The patient logs the date and time her period started using an automated WhatsApp message or voice prompt.
- Dynamic date calculation: The intake system calculates the eligible appointment window based on the reported cycle start date.
- Slot filtering: The workflow checks live clinic calendars and presents only open slots that fall strictly within her eligible window.
- Instant calendar hold: When the patient selects a slot, the system confirms the booking in the clinic calendar and delivers automated pre-test instructions.

This automated date-matching logic removes mental arithmetic from front-desk staff, eliminates multiple phone calls, and secures the booking within minutes of the patient's period starting.

## Co-ordinating Shared Suites: Radiologist and Gynaecologist Rosters

Unlike regular gynaecological checkups or pelvic ultrasounds, an HSG requires specialised imaging equipment. Specifically, the clinic or hospital must use a fluoroscopy suite. This creates an operational challenge involving shared rooms and doctor schedules.

During an HSG, the patient lies on an examination table under a fluoroscope. Either a gynaecologist or a radiologist places the speculum in the vagina, carries out the cervical cannulation, and instils the contrast medium under fluoroscopy ([Hysterosalpingogram (HSG)](https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/hysterosalpingogram-hsg/)).

To prevent suite idle time and avoid delays for patients, clinic scheduling systems must synchronise three independent resources at once:

- Gynaecologist procedural roster: Aligning appointment times with the gynaecologist's schedule as they move between OPD consultations, minor surgery lists, and diagnostic tests.
- Radiologist availability: Coordinating with the radiologist's duty roster so fluoroscopy reporting and imaging work proceed smoothly.
- Fluoroscopy suite availability: Reserving the imaging room and recovery bed while accounting for sanitation and patient monitoring.

Central multi-calendar booking ensures that whenever a patient selects an eligible date, the system reserves both the clinician and the fluoroscopy room. This coordination prevents double bookings and keeps diagnostic rooms running on time.

## Standardising Pre-Procedure Clinical Prep and Contrast Screening

Securing an appointment slot is only the first step. Ensuring patient adherence to preparation instructions is equally critical to prevent cancellations on the day of the test. Giving instructions verbally over a noisy phone call often leads to forgotten steps. Automated WhatsApp messages deliver structured guidance at the right clinical intervals.

### 1\. Prophylactic Antibiotic Timing

Healthcare providers may direct patients to take prophylactic antibiotics beforehand to prevent infection ([Hysterosalpingogram (HSG): Preparation, Procedure & Results](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diagnostics/22254-hysterosalpingogram)). In some cases, patients are prescribed antibiotics to take both before and after the HSG ([Hysterosalpingography (HSG)](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/hysterosalpingography)). Sending an automated WhatsApp reminder 24 hours prior to the test prompts the patient to collect her prescription and take her medication exactly as directed by her doctor.

### 2\. Pre-Medication for Cramping

Uterine cramping during dye instillation is the most common discomfort during an HSG. Healthcare providers may recommend taking an over-the-counter pain reliever one hour before the procedure ([Hysterosalpingography (HSG)](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/hysterosalpingography), [Hysterosalpingogram (HSG): Preparation, Procedure & Results](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diagnostics/22254-hysterosalpingogram)). Medications commonly used for menstrual cramps, like ibuprofen or other over-the-counter NSAIDs, can significantly reduce mild to moderate uterine cramping ([Hysterosalpingogram (HSG)](https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/hysterosalpingogram-hsg/), [Hysterosalpingogram (HSG): Preparation, Procedure & Results](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diagnostics/22254-hysterosalpingogram)). Clinics can schedule an automated WhatsApp alert 90 minutes before the appointment, giving the patient enough time to take her medication before travelling to the clinic.

### 3\. Contrast Allergy Screening

HSG procedures routinely use contrast solutions. Patients who are allergic to iodine must have the HSG performed using a contrast medium that does not contain iodine ([Hysterosalpingogram (HSG)](https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/hysterosalpingogram-hsg/)). Incorporating digital allergy screening into the initial booking sequence flags these sensitivities days in advance. This allows the radiology team to prepare non-iodinated contrast solutions before the patient arrives.

## Alleviating Procedural Anxiety with Multilingual Conversational AI

Last-minute cancellations and no-shows for HSG appointments are frequently driven by anxiety rather than simple forgetfulness. Patients searching online often encounter dramatic stories about pain during the test. Without direct guidance from the clinic, fear can prompt patients to postpone or skip their booking.

Receptionists rarely have the time during busy morning clinics to spend fifteen minutes answering questions and reassuring an anxious patient. Conversational voice AI and automated messaging can bridge this gap by answering common questions promptly and accurately.

Automated tools deliver reassurance based on documented clinical guidance:

- Pain and cramping duration: Uterine cramping during or after the procedure is typically mild to moderate ([Hysterosalpingogram (HSG)](https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/hysterosalpingogram-hsg/), [Hysterosalpingogram (HSG): Preparation, Procedure & Results](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diagnostics/22254-hysterosalpingogram)). It usually lasts between 5 and 10 minutes ([Hysterosalpingogram (HSG)](https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/hysterosalpingogram-hsg/)), though it can sometimes continue for a few hours ([Hysterosalpingogram (HSG): Preparation, Procedure & Results](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diagnostics/22254-hysterosalpingogram)). Taking medications used for menstrual cramps, like ibuprofen or over-the-counter NSAIDs, helps reduce this discomfort ([Hysterosalpingogram (HSG)](https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/hysterosalpingogram-hsg/), [Hysterosalpingogram (HSG): Preparation, Procedure & Results](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diagnostics/22254-hysterosalpingogram)).
- Local anaesthetic options: Patients can speak with their healthcare provider about local anaesthesia options, such as numbing creams, gels, or injections applied to the cervix right before the procedure ([Hysterosalpingography (HSG)](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/hysterosalpingography)).
- Radiation safety: Radiation exposure from an HSG exam is very low, and clinics confirm that the procedure is not performed if pregnancy is suspected ([Hysterosalpingogram (HSG)](https://www.reproductivefacts.org/news-and-publications/fact-sheets-and-infographics/hysterosalpingogram-hsg/)).

When a patient receives prompt answers in her preferred language—such as Hindi, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu, or Indian English—procedural apprehension drops, helping reduce same-day cancellations.

## Frequently asked questions

### When should an HSG be scheduled in a menstrual cycle?

An HSG is best scheduled during the first half of the menstrual cycle between days 1 and 14, after menstrual bleeding has ended but before ovulation occurs. Healthcare providers ideally schedule the test between cycle days 7 and 10 to minimise the risk of an undetected pregnancy.

### What conditions prevent a patient from undergoing an HSG?

An HSG must not be performed if pregnancy is suspected, if the patient has active uterine bleeding, or if there is an active pelvic infection. It should also be avoided in patients with moderate to severe contrast dye allergies, while those with iodine allergies require non-iodinated contrast media.

### How can clinics help patients prepare for HSG cramping?

Clinics can remind patients that uterine cramping is typically mild to moderate and often lasts between 5 and 10 minutes. Healthcare providers may recommend taking an over-the-counter pain reliever, such as ibuprofen or other NSAIDs, one hour before the appointment, or discuss local anaesthesia options.

### Why do clinics need multi-calendar booking for HSG appointments?

An HSG requires synchronising three separate resources: the gynaecologist performing the procedure, the radiologist overseeing imaging, and the fluoroscopy suite. Multi-calendar booking reserves both specialists and the room simultaneously, preventing double bookings and delays.
