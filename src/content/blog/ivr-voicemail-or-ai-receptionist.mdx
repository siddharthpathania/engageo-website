Someone rings your clinic at 8:20 PM with a swollen jaw. The desk shut at seven. What happens in the next twelve seconds decides whether that person becomes an appointment on Thursday or a walk-in two lanes over.

You have three machine answers for those twelve seconds: voicemail, an IVR menu, or an AI voice agent. They are not three grades of the same thing. They break differently, and what suits a two-chair dental clinic is rarely what suits a ninety-bed hospital with eight departments.

Five things decide it: the caller's experience, Indian languages, cost shape, setup effort, and what happens when a call is not routine.

## What a caller actually does inside a voice menu

The best evidence on how people behave when a machine asks them to press buttons comes from health research, not telecom marketing. A feasibility study of an IVR patient-satisfaction survey in Ethiopia enrolled 2,415 patients. Of those, 2,236, or 92.6%, picked up. Only 1,584, or 65.6%, answered even one question. And 1,037, or 42.9%, reached the end of the fifteen questions ([Using an Interactive Voice Response Survey to Assess Patient Satisf…](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11892327/)).

Where did the rest go? 400 people, 16.6%, listened to the introduction and hung up. Another 252 heard the first question and did not answer. And 339, 14% of everyone enrolled, were disconnected by the system itself for giving invalid responses ([Using an Interactive Voice Response Survey to Assess Patient Satisf…](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11892327/)).

Hold on to that last group. One in seven did not fail to reply. They replied, and the machine refused what they said. Your desk would have understood them.

That was a research survey in Amharic and Afaan Oromo, with consenting patients, fifteen questions long ([Using an Interactive Voice Response Survey to Assess Patient Satisf…](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11892327/)). A clinic call is shorter. But the shape carries: every step loses people, and a rejected input loses them for good.

## The three options, plainly

### Voicemail

Cheapest and most honest. It also assumes something usually untrue in India: that a patient in discomfort will speak to a beep and then wait.

Count two numbers from last month before you rely on it: voicemails received, and voicemails your desk actually returned. If the second is not close to the first, voicemail is not an answering system. It is a queue you are not clearing. Its real place is the last step, after opening hours and a WhatsApp number have been stated.

### The IVR menu

A sorting machine, and at sorting it is genuinely good. If your callers fall into departments, pharmacy, reports, billing, appointments, and most know which they want, a two-option menu sorts them without anyone picking up.

It is bad at the thing that matters most: urgency. A person in pain does not want a taxonomy. Menus also grow by accretion. Someone adds an option for insurance, someone adds one for the second branch, and a year later a first-time caller hears six choices before any mention of a doctor. Three rules keep one usable. Commonest reason first. Never deeper than two levels. Always offer a human, and say so in the first sentence.

### An AI voice agent

It removes the menu. The caller says what they want in their own words, the system checks the calendar, and either books a slot or takes a structured message.

Two things nothing else offers. It holds a conversation out of order, so a caller who blurts out "Dr Mehta, Saturday morning, follow-up" is not pushed back to step one. And it writes into your calendar, so the call ends with a slot, not a promise that somebody will ring back.

It also brings a failure mode the others cannot. Voicemail never misunderstands you. An IVR can only mishear a keypress. A voice agent can be confidently wrong.

## Language is where this gets decided in India

A benchmark called Voice of India tested speech recognition on unscripted telephone conversations across 15 major Indian languages and 139 regional clusters, using 306,230 utterances, 536 hours of audio and 36,691 speakers ([Voice of India: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Real-World Speech Recog…](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19151)). Real calls, not studio reading.

The results are uneven. District-level word error rates ran from roughly 4% in one district to 44% in another ([Voice of India: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Real-World Speech Recog…](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19151)). The paper treats a word error rate of 20 as the threshold associated with practical usability, and no system it tested stayed below that across every language. Even the strongest performer crossed it on lower-resource languages, reaching 20.9% on Bhojpuri and 24.8% on Maithili ([Voice of India: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Real-World Speech Recog…](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19151)). Some general-purpose models failed outright on particular languages, one scoring 295.9% word error rate on Gujarati ([Voice of India: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Real-World Speech Recog…](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19151)).

Two further findings matter. Models that score well on clean scripted benchmarks often do substantially worse on unscripted speech, and degraded audio, faster speech and very short utterances all pushed error rates up ([Voice of India: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Real-World Speech Recog…](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19151)). A patient calling from a lift in Ghaziabad is all three at once.

So the real test is not "does it support Hindi". Have three staff who speak the languages your patients speak call in from their own phones on a weekday afternoon, and read the transcripts. If your mix leans on a lower-resource language, an IVR with a language choice at step one may be the more truthful option today.

## Cost shape, not sticker price

The three price on different units, which matters more than the headline figure.

- Voicemail is near-fixed. You pay for the line. The real cost is staff time returning messages.
- An IVR is mostly fixed too: a monthly fee plus call minutes. A fourth department costs configuration effort, not money.
- A voice agent is usually per minute or per call, so the bill tracks volume. Festival weeks cost more.

If your fallback is a WhatsApp text-back rather than a voice conversation, the arithmetic changes again. WhatsApp Business Platform billing moved to per-message pricing on 1 July 2025, and you are charged when a marketing, utility or authentication template is delivered ([Pricing - WhatsApp Business Platform](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)). When a patient messages you first, a customer service window opens for 24 hours, and inside it non-template messages and utility templates are not charged ([Pricing - WhatsApp Business Platform](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)). That rewards one design: get the patient to message first, then finish the booking inside the open window.

## Setup effort and the rules you inherit

Voicemail is quick to set up. An IVR takes longer than vendors suggest, and most of that time goes on arguing about the menu tree and recording prompts in each language. A voice agent is the slowest of the three: it needs your calendar, your doctors' real availability, your fee list and a written escalation rule.

There is a compliance layer under all three that clinics discover late. TRAI treats commercial communication as something that may only go out on registered headers assigned to the sender, and it registers telemarketers by function, including a voice-calling function whose operators must protect Preference Register and Consent Register data ([Advice to Telemarketers](https://www.trai.gov.in/advice-telemarketers)). Promotional messages need explicit consent, while service messages ride on consent or a registered template ([Advice to Telemarketers](https://www.trai.gov.in/advice-telemarketers)). A booking confirmation is a different animal from an offer on dental cleaning. Do not let one system send both under the same assumptions.

Whoever you buy from, Engageo included, ask for one artefact before signing: a transcript of a call the system got wrong. A vendor who cannot show you a failure has not looked for one.

## Which one to pick

### A small clinic, one or two doctors

Skip the IVR. Your callers do not need sorting, because there is only one place they can go. Your problem is coverage: evenings, lunch, and the fifteen minutes when the receptionist is at the bank.

Take a voice agent that books straight into the calendar, or a missed-call text-back on WhatsApp, and keep voicemail last. Start the agent narrow, new appointment and reschedule only, and read every transcript yourself for the first fortnight.

### A multi-doctor hospital or a chain

Sorting is a real problem here, so an IVR is not dead weight. The strongest arrangement is usually both: a short menu for the department split, and a voice agent behind the appointments option that books into the right doctor's calendar. Measure per branch. One location with a different language mix will drag the average around and hide a fault you could have fixed.

## The one call none of these should own

Set the emergency rule before anything goes live, and make it the first thing any system says. Under the National Health Mission, 108 runs predominantly as an emergency response system for critical care, trauma and accident victims, while 102 provides basic patient transport aimed at pregnant women and children, and 35 states and union territories have this dial-for-an-ambulance facility ([ERS/Patient Transport Service](https://nhm.gov.in/nhm_live/index1.php?lang=1&level=2&sublinkid=1217&lid=189)).

Say it plainly, in the caller's language: for an emergency, go to the nearest hospital or dial 108. No automated system should be triaging a person in crisis, or asking them to press 4.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do we know whether the voice agent understands our patients?

Read transcripts, not dashboards. Pull thirty calls a week for the first month, in each language your patients use, and mark each understood, misunderstood or escalated. Recognition quality varies a great deal by language and audio conditions ([Voice of India: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Real-World Speech Recog…](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19151)), so one accuracy figure hides the group being failed.

### What should happen when the agent cannot handle a call?

Decide in advance and write it down. The sensible default is a clean handover: the system says someone will call back, records who and why, and puts it on a named person's list with a time. A promise nobody owns is worse than an honest "we open at 9 AM".
