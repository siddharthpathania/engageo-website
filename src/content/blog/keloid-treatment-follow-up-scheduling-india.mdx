Treating keloids in Indian dermatology clinics involves clear medical steps alongside operational challenges. When a patient arrives with an active scar, the doctor provides [intralesional steroid therapy](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/intralesional-injection). This treatment calms itching and softens firm tissue. However, steroid injections are rarely a single visit. Clinical protocols show that injections can be repeated every 4 to 6 weeks for 2 to 3 cycles or longer ([Intralesional injection](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/intralesional-injection)), or spaced at 3 to 6-week intervals ([Intralesional drug therapy in dermatology](https://ijdvl.com/intralesional-drug-therapy-in-dermatology/)).

A common issue in Indian outpatient practices is early patient drop-off. Many patients stop coming after one or two sessions. When itching stops and tenderness fades, patients think the scar is cured. In reality, the keloid has only started to respond. If the patient abandons treatment early, the scar tissue can quickly grow back.

Reception bottlenecks make this problem worse. Front desks stay crowded during busy outpatient department (OPD) hours. Receptionists juggle in-person billing and non-stop phone calls. When a patient calls to move a visit and the phone rings out, they rarely call back. A planned monthly injection turns into months of missed care. Dermatology practices need simple workflows and automated tools to keep patients on track.

## Clinical Injection Protocols and Recommended Interval Timelines

A good recall system must follow proven clinical rules. [Triamcinolone acetonide](https://ijdvl.com/intralesional-drug-therapy-in-dermatology/) is widely used to treat keloids ([Intralesional drug therapy in dermatology](https://ijdvl.com/intralesional-drug-therapy-in-dermatology/)). Good results depend on exact dosing, correct injection depth, and regular calendar intervals.

Clinical studies define clear benchmarks for dosing, volumes, and visit spacing:

- Injection Intervals: Injections are repeated once every 3 to 4 weeks based on scar bulk and clinical response ([Standard guidelines of care: Keloids and hypertrophic scars](https://ijdvl.com/standard-guidelines-of-care-keloids-and-hypertrophic-scars/)). Other protocols use intervals of 3 to 6 weeks ([Intralesional drug therapy in dermatology](https://ijdvl.com/intralesional-drug-therapy-in-dermatology/)), or repeat every 4 to 6 weeks for 2 to 3 cycles or longer ([Intralesional injection](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/intralesional-injection)).
- Drug Concentrations: Triamcinolone acetonide is often used at 10 to 20 mg/ml for keloids ([Standard guidelines of care: Keloids and hypertrophic scars](https://ijdvl.com/standard-guidelines-of-care-keloids-and-hypertrophic-scars/)). Concentrations of 2.5 to 10 mg/ml are also documented for keloids ([Intralesional drug therapy in dermatology](https://ijdvl.com/intralesional-drug-therapy-in-dermatology/)), while 5 to 10 mg/ml is used for moderate-thickness hypertrophic scars ([Intralesional injection](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/intralesional-injection)). Tough, bulky lesions can receive up to 40 mg/ml ([Intralesional injection](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/intralesional-injection), [Standard guidelines of care: Keloids and hypertrophic scars](https://ijdvl.com/standard-guidelines-of-care-keloids-and-hypertrophic-scars/)).
- Dosing Volume: [Standard guidelines](https://ijdvl.com/standard-guidelines-of-care-keloids-and-hypertrophic-scars/) suggest 0.1 to 0.2 ml per square centimetre of involved skin ([Intralesional injection](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/intralesional-injection), [Intralesional drug therapy in dermatology](https://ijdvl.com/intralesional-drug-therapy-in-dermatology/)). The total dose per session should generally stay under 1 to 2 ml, and the maximum dose must not exceed 40 mg/ml per session ([Intralesional drug therapy in dermatology](https://ijdvl.com/intralesional-drug-therapy-in-dermatology/)).
- Injection Depth: Doctors must inject the steroid at the correct depth in the mid-dermis to prevent irreversible epidermal atrophy ([Standard guidelines of care: Keloids and hypertrophic scars](https://ijdvl.com/standard-guidelines-of-care-keloids-and-hypertrophic-scars/)). Injections into subcutaneous tissue increase the likelihood of atrophy ([Intralesional injection](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/intralesional-injection)).

Leaving follow-up dates open ruins these treatment plans. Clinics should treat the follow-up appointment as a mandatory part of procedural care.

## Addressing the Clinical and Behavioural Causes of Patient Drop-Off

Understanding why patients drop out helps clinics prevent it. Patients rarely skip visits out of malice. Instead, predictable physical discomfort and confusion lead to missed appointments.

### Procedure-Related Injection Discomfort

Pain during the injection is an important limiting factor in steroid therapy ([Standard guidelines of care: Keloids and hypertrophic scars](https://ijdvl.com/standard-guidelines-of-care-keloids-and-hypertrophic-scars/)). Keloid tissue is tough, fibrous, and dense. Injecting fluid into tight scar tissue creates pressure and sharp discomfort. When clinics do not prepare patients for this pain, patients avoid booking their next session.

### Symptom Relief Confused with Scar Resolution

Keloids frequently itch, ache, and sting. Steroid injections relieve these surface symptoms within a few days. The scar softens, but deep collagen bundles remain active. Patients think the scar is cured and stop coming. Without full suppression, the scar rebounds.

### Unmonitored Local Reactions and Anxiety

Steroid injections can cause short-term local pain and mild inflammatory reactions ([Intralesional injection](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/intralesional-injection)). A patient might notice redness or soreness a day after treatment. If they cannot reach the front desk to ask questions, they worry. That worry often leads to cancelled visits.

## Keloid Treatment Follow Up Scheduling: Automated Interval Recalls and Pre-Booked Cycles

To keep patients on schedule, front desks must remove manual friction. Asking a patient to call back in a month rarely works. Busy lives cause people to forget. Clinics need an automated system that secures the next slot before the patient walks out the door.

An effective follow-up workflow uses three clear steps:

1. Mandatory Checkout Scheduling: The front-desk team books the next injection at the billing desk during checkout. The patient leaves with a confirmed date in hand.
2. Automated WhatsApp Touchpoints: Confirmed visits trigger instant WhatsApp confirmations. Automated reminders follow at 24 hours and 3 hours before the visit, reducing no-shows.
3. Automated Overdue Recalls: If a patient cancels or misses a date, the software flags them right away. A simple four-touchpoint messaging sequence over seven days prompts them to pick a new slot.

Automated calendar reminders remove manual ledger tracking. The front desk saves time, and doctor schedules remain predictable.

## Delivering Timed Patient Education to Mitigate Drop-Out and Complications

Patients return when they understand why repeat visits matter. Automated messaging lets clinics educate patients without taking up doctor consultation time.

Automated messages sent to the patient should cover several key facts:

- Separating Symptom Relief from Healing: A message sent a week after injection reminds the patient that lack of itching does not mean the scar has flattened permanently.
- Explaining Normal Reactions and Side Effects: Patients should know what to expect. Corticosteroids can cause short-term local pain and local inflammation ([Intralesional injection](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/intralesional-injection)). Documented adverse effects include atrophy, telangiectasia, and pigment changes ([Intralesional injection](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/intralesional-injection), [Intralesional drug therapy in dermatology](https://ijdvl.com/intralesional-drug-therapy-in-dermatology/)). In some cases, combining triamcinolone with 5-fluorouracil leads to less skin atrophy and telangiectasia than steroid therapy alone ([Standard guidelines of care: Keloids and hypertrophic scars](https://ijdvl.com/standard-guidelines-of-care-keloids-and-hypertrophic-scars/)). Sharing this context eases worry.
- Post-Procedure Care Advice: A text sent the next morning can outline simple home care for local soreness. Knowing what is normal keeps patients calm.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the recommended interval for keloid steroid injections?

Clinical protocols recommend repeating injections every 3 to 4 weeks depending on scar bulk, or spacing sessions at intervals of 3 to 6 weeks for multiple cycles.

### Why do patients frequently discontinue keloid injection treatments early?

Patients often mistake early symptom relief such as reduced itching for scar resolution, experience procedural pain during injections, or worry over mild local inflammatory reactions.

### What steroid concentrations and dosages are used for keloid treatment?

Triamcinolone acetonide is commonly administered at concentrations between 2.5 and 20 mg/ml, with up to 40 mg/ml used for bulky lesions, typically delivered at 0.1 to 0.2 ml per square centimetre.

### How can Indian dermatology clinics prevent follow-up drop-offs?
