Operating theatres (OTs) are the clinical heart and primary revenue engine of any Indian multi-specialty hospital. Yet hospital administrators and medical superintendents face constant schedule disruptions. Morning-of-surgery delays and last-minute cancellations remain everyday headaches. More often than not, the delay is not caused by a sudden emergency or broken equipment. It happens because of an incomplete, missing, or delayed pre-anaesthesia check-up (PAC).

Elective surgery patients often arrive at the hospital admissions desk on the morning of their procedure unprepared. The on-duty anaesthetist may discover unreviewed heart conditions, missing blood reports, or broken fasting rules. When an elective list breaks down at 8:00 AM, the entire hospital feels the impact. Sterile trays sit unused, nursing rotas stall, and surgeons wait around with empty theatre time.

This problem stems from a fragmented process. A surgeon advises an operation in an outpatient department (OPD), hands over a slip, and expects the patient to finish pre-op clearances independently. Paper slips get lost, and patients miss critical steps. Modernising pre anaesthesia check up appointment scheduling through automated WhatsApp workflows bridges this operational void. It keeps surgeons, diagnostic labs, anaesthesiologists, and patients aligned from the first consult to the operating table.

## Where Pre-Anaesthesia Check-Up Scheduling Breaks Down in Multi-Specialty OPDs

In busy hospital OPDs, surgeons often recommend elective surgery and book an operating table before fitness is evaluated. The [Indian Society of Anaesthesiologists (ISA) recommends](https://www.isaweb.in/Pdf/ISA-PPF-GUIDELINES.pdf) that surgeons request PAC clearance from the anaesthesiologist before listing patients for the operating room ([ISA Guidelines for Anaesthesiologists in India](https://www.isaweb.in/Pdf/ISA-PPF-GUIDELINES.pdf)). In daily practice, however, this sequence is often reversed.

ISA guidelines advise anaesthesiologists to establish their own pre-anaesthesia clinic in each hospital, or at least visit the patient preoperatively and in the postoperative period ([ISA Guidelines for Anaesthesiologists in India](https://www.isaweb.in/Pdf/ISA-PPF-GUIDELINES.pdf)). Even so, paper-based coordination causes patients to drop off at several friction points:

- Confusing OPD navigation: Patients leave surgical consultations with handwritten slips. They get lost between billing counters, diagnostic collection centres, and registration desks without booking their PAC slot.
- Long clinic queues: Walk-in PAC clinics create unpredictable rushes. Patients wait for hours in OPD corridors and often leave before seeing the anaesthetist.
- Repeat visits: Patients frequently reach the anaesthetist without the right test results. The doctor must order tests and ask for a second visit, delaying the surgical date.
- No central visibility: Surgical coordinators and OT managers cannot see which listed patients have achieved medical fitness until physical files arrive on surgery morning.

Relying on manual patient initiative creates drop-offs between the initial surgical recommendation and the final operating theatre schedule.

## Automating Diagnostic Prerequisites and Multilingual Patient Questionnaires

Unprepared patients frequently cause delays in PAC clinics. Anaesthesiologists cannot clear a case without an accurate medical history and basic diagnostic reports. Yet patients often arrive empty-handed or unable to recall past health problems.

ISA guidelines recommend using an anaesthesia-specific consent form alongside a pre-operative questionnaire given to the patient in their local language once scheduled for surgery ([ISA Guidelines for Anaesthesiologists in India](https://www.isaweb.in/Pdf/ISA-PPF-GUIDELINES.pdf)). The guidelines also instruct that this questionnaire be filled by the patient in their own handwriting with the help of a relative or guardian, and that the patient bring all previous medical records to the anaesthesiologist ([ISA Guidelines for Anaesthesiologists in India](https://www.isaweb.in/Pdf/ISA-PPF-GUIDELINES.pdf)).

Hospitals can eliminate repeat visits by digitising and automating this preparation over WhatsApp:

- Digital questionnaire delivery: ISA guidelines advise providing a pre-operative questionnaire in the patient's local language once surgery is scheduled ([ISA Guidelines for Anaesthesiologists in India](https://www.isaweb.in/Pdf/ISA-PPF-GUIDELINES.pdf)). When an OPD registers a procedure, automated WhatsApp messages can deliver this questionnaire directly to the patient.
- Clear medical history: Simple mobile prompts help family members note down chronic illnesses, past anaesthesia reactions, current medications, and known drug allergies before visiting the hospital.
- Automated lab reminders: Timed alerts remind patients to complete essential baseline tests, such as complete blood counts, coagulation profiles, renal function tests, ECGs, and chest X-rays, before their PAC slot.
- Record consolidation: Automated messages remind patients to bring previous discharge summaries, current prescription strips, and cardiology clearances directly to their appointment.

Having tests and medical records ready before the patient enters the clinic turns a long administrative search into a quick, decisive clinical assessment.

## Synchronising Anaesthesiologist Rosters with Primary OT Schedules

A common structural hurdle in Indian hospitals is the disconnect between operating theatre booking systems and anaesthesiologist duty rotas. Surgeons book theatre slots based on their own availability, assuming an anaesthetist will be available to assess and clear the patient in time.

An automated scheduling setup links surgeon bookings, dedicated PAC clinic hours, and anaesthetist duty rotas in a unified calendar. This calendar sync avoids costly scheduling errors.

### Dynamic Slot Allocation Based on Surgery Timelines

Automated workflows can count backwards from the planned surgery date to allocate an ideal PAC slot. For routine elective procedures, booking PAC slots a few days before surgery gives adequate time to review lab results while keeping clinical findings current.

### Buffer Windows for Specialty Referrals

Elderly patients and those with complex medical histories often need additional clearances, such as 2D echocardiography, cardiology opinions, or chest physician reviews. Manual workflows usually catch these needs on admission morning, causing sudden postponements. Smart scheduling flags high-risk cases right at the OPD stage. It books earlier PAC appointments to leave enough buffer time for external specialist consultations.

## Delivering Timed Fasting Guidelines and Pre-Op Instructions via WhatsApp

Broken fasting instructions are a primary trigger for day-of-surgery delays and cancellations. At the same time, overly strict instructions like 'do not eat or drink after midnight' leave patients dehydrated, weak, and stressed.

The Indian Society of Anaesthesiologists has published detailed [practice guidelines for perioperative fasting](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413358/) in adults and paediatric patients ([Perioperative fasting and feeding in adults, obstetric, paediatric…](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413358/)).

### Adult Perioperative Fasting Standards

- Clear liquids: Allowed up to 2 hours before sedation or anaesthesia, preferably limited to less than 450 mL ([Perioperative fasting and feeding in adults, obstetric, paediatric…](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413358/)).
- Non-clear liquids: Allowed up to 4 hours before sedation or anaesthesia ([Perioperative fasting and feeding in adults, obstetric, paediatric…](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413358/)).
- Light meals: Allowed up to 6 hours before sedation or anaesthesia ([Perioperative fasting and feeding in adults, obstetric, paediatric…](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413358/)).
- Heavy meals: Patients must wait at least 10 hours after eating heavy meals before receiving sedation or anaesthesia ([Perioperative fasting and feeding in adults, obstetric, paediatric…](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413358/)).

Furthermore, ISA guidelines state that routine aspiration prophylaxis is not advised in adequately fasted adult patients ([Perioperative fasting and feeding in adults, obstetric, paediatric…](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413358/)). Prophylactic medications like H2 receptor blockers, proton pump inhibitors, and prokinetics are reserved for high-risk patients identified by the anaesthesiologist ([Perioperative fasting and feeding in adults, obstetric, paediatric…](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413358/)).

### Paediatric Perioperative Fasting Standards

- Plain water: Allowed up to 1 hour before anaesthesia, up to a volume of 3 mL/kg ([Perioperative fasting and feeding in adults, obstetric, paediatric…](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413358/)).
- Clear liquids other than water: Allowed up to 2 hours before anaesthesia, up to 3 mL/kg ([Perioperative fasting and feeding in adults, obstetric, paediatric…](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413358/)).
- Breast milk and fully skimmed non-human milk: Allowed up to 4 hours before anaesthesia ([Perioperative fasting and feeding in adults, obstetric, paediatric…](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413358/)).
- Non-clear liquids, formula, non-human milk, and light meals: Require a 6-hour fasting window before anaesthesia ([Perioperative fasting and feeding in adults, obstetric, paediatric…](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7413358/)).

Hospitals can tie automated WhatsApp alerts to the patient's exact scheduled theatre time. For example, an adult booked for surgery at 11:00 AM receives an automated reminder to finish a light breakfast before 5:00 AM. They get an alert permitting clear water up to 450 mL until 9:00 AM, followed by strict fasting. This avoids confusion, protects patient comfort, and prevents cancellations.

## Connecting Attending Surgical Units and Patients with Real-Time Alerts

A reliable pre-anaesthesia workflow requires smooth, two-way communication between the PAC clinic, the patient, and the primary surgical unit.

Under ISA guidelines, anaesthesiologists must ensure the availability of and perform an anaesthesia safety checklist before administering anaesthesia ([ISA Guidelines for Anaesthesiologists in India](https://www.isaweb.in/Pdf/ISA-PPF-GUIDELINES.pdf)). Digitising the communication loop helps confirm these safety prerequisites well before the patient reaches the holding bay.

### Instant Clearance Notifications to Surgical Teams

When an anaesthetist marks a patient as fit in the hospital system, an automated notification should alert the primary surgical coordinator on WhatsApp immediately. If fitness is withheld due to high blood sugar or pending cardiac tests, the surgical unit learns about it right away. This allows staff to reallocate the theatre slot instead of finding out on operation day.

### Two-Way Patient Acknowledgements

Automated WhatsApp messages sent the evening before surgery can prompt patients to tap a button confirming their arrival time and fasting compliance. If a patient reports a sudden fever or an accidental snack, theatre managers get immediate notice. They can reorder the surgical list without leaving operating rooms idle.

## Frequently asked questions

### What fasting rules should adult patients follow before anaesthesia?

According to Indian Society of Anaesthesiologists guidelines, adults may have clear liquids up to 2 hours before anaesthesia (preferably under 450 mL), non-clear liquids up to 4 hours, light meals up to 6 hours, and heavy meals up to 10 hours before anaesthesia.

### How are paediatric fasting guidelines structured under ISA standards?

Paediatric guidelines allow plain water up to 1 hour before anaesthesia (up to 3 mL/kg) and clear liquids up to 2 hours (up to 3 mL/kg). Breast milk and fully skimmed non-human milk are allowed up to 4 hours, while non-clear liquids, formula, and light meals require a 6-hour fasting window.

### How does automated WhatsApp scheduling prevent day-of-surgery cancellations?

Automated WhatsApp workflows deliver pre-operative questionnaires, remind patients of required diagnostic tests, and send timed fasting alerts aligned with the theatre schedule. They also send immediate clearance updates to surgical coordinators so unready cases are resolved well before surgery day.
